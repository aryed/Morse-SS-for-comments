# Mathematical Writing

Infer the task, audience, and requested scope from the request and available manuscript. Proceed without asking the user to choose a module. Read enough surrounding text to preserve notation, hypotheses, and the passage's role; do not require a whole paper for a local edit.

## Shared core

- Preserve mathematical meaning, hypotheses, quantifiers, conventions, claim status, and the author's calm voice. Distinguish established results, conjectures, heuristic explanations, and gaps. Do not turn an omitted proof into a proved assertion by polishing it.
- Make the main point apparent early at the relevant scale. Explain conceptual difficulty and nonroutine transitions; compress genuinely routine steps for the intended reader. Brevity is useful only when it preserves readability.
- Write complete mathematical sentences with explicit logical relationships. Introduce notation near its use and recall the meaning of unfamiliar symbols. Preserve established manuscript macros and conventions.
- Prefer the simplest useful level of generality. Do not add motivation, examples, lemmas, applications, or a new organizational scheme merely to satisfy a checklist.
- Treat advice as conditional heuristics. Explicit user requests and manuscript requirements govern structure and typography. A short note need not acquire an abstract; an existing paper need not acquire new packages or counters.
- For grammar/spelling-only requests, correct only those issues and load no additional modules. Do not silently repair mathematics; if a serious issue must be flagged, mention it separately and briefly.
- For rewriting, keep editorial changes distinct from proposed mathematical changes. Verify a proposed proof simplification before substituting it; otherwise present it as a possible direction with the missing step.
- Use accurate, specific references where claims depend on specialized literature. Never fabricate a citation or locator. Mark unresolved references as draft gaps when necessary.

## Finish proportionately

Check that the output fulfills the requested purpose, preserves meaning, and exposes unresolved mathematical issues. For a draft, deliver the draft; for critique, deliver concrete prioritized findings. Keep explanations of edits short unless requested. Do not imply a full mathematical verification from an editorial pass.
